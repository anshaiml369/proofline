# Hackathon Frameworks (from masterclass — every pitch sentence must answer one)

## Wispr Flow's Four Questions
- 01 PROBLEM & USER: Who has the pain? What specifically is difficult?
- 02 FREQUENCY & SCALE: How often does it happen? How many people face it?
- 03 WHY NOW? WHY VOICE?: Why is this possible today? Where does voice help?
- 04 ALTERNATIVES: What is the current workaround? Why would users switch?

## Marty Cagan's Four Risks
- VALUE: Will people choose to use it?
- USABILITY: Can people figure out how to use it?
- FEASIBILITY: Can we build and operate it?
- VIABILITY: Can we reach users and make it sustainable? (HIGHLIGHTED in masterclass — they care most about this)

## Mark Pincus: Proven → Better → New
- Start with the user's current workaround
- The new workflow must be demonstrably better
- The "new" part is what you add on top

## Three Non-Negotiables
1. "One tiny core that is so exceptionally good" (Max Schoening, Notion)
2. "Try 100 things, throw out 99, ship 1" (Ian Silber, OpenAI)
3. "Where will our first ten users come from?" (Evan Spiegel)

## Two Rules
1. Never claim "no competitors." Name them, state your wedge.
2. "Trust but verify" (Fiona Fung) — the AI does not decide compliance. Fixed rules do.
